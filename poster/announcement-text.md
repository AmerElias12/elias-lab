## 1 · Main LinkedIn post — opening + MSc + collaboration

> **The Elias Lab opens this October at Braude College of Engineering, Karmiel.**
>
> Most genome editing works by breaking DNA. CRISPR, ZFNs and TALENs cut, then
> rely on the cell to repair. That's powerful for small edits — but it struggles
> to insert large sequences, and every break carries risk: indels, translocations,
> unintended activation.
>
> We're taking a different route.
>
> Our lab engineers **tyrosine site-specific recombinases** — enzymes that
> recognise a target sequence and integrate DNA directly into it, with no
> double-strand break at all. Our work centres on the HK022 integrase and on the
> ~40,000 native attB-like sites mapped across the human genome.
>
> What that makes possible: multi-kilobase payloads, delivered to chosen loci,
> with a single therapeutic strategy that can work across patients carrying
> different mutations in the same gene.
>
> Three directions we're pursuing:
> → Discovery, engineering and optimisation of novel recombinases
> → Gene therapy for hereditary disease and for cancer
> → Cell-line engineering and stable model development
>
> **🎓 I'm recruiting MSc students.**
> You'd join as part of the founding team — real ownership of a project, not a
> corner of someone else's. Projects span protein engineering and directed
> evolution, CRISPR and cell-line engineering, molecular assay development and
> computational design. Backgrounds in molecular biology, biochemistry,
> biotechnology engineering or bioinformatics all fit. Curiosity and rigour
> matter more than a flawless CV.
>
> **🤝 And I'm open to collaboration.**
> If you work on a disease where inserting a large payload is the bottleneck —
> or you need engineered cell lines, CRISPR libraries, plasmid construction or
> custom molecular assays — I'd like to talk. We offer these to academic and
> industry partners, and already work with university, clinical and industry
> groups across Israel.
>
> 📩 amerelias02@gmail.com
>
