### About the lab

The Elias Lab opens in October 2026 within the Biotechnology Engineering
Department at Braude College of Engineering. We develop tools for **precise,
large-scale DNA integration into the human genome without creating
double-strand breaks** — an alternative foundation for gene therapy to the
cut-and-repair logic that current editors depend on.

The lab builds on published work on the HK022 bacteriophage integrase, including
recombinase-mediated cassette exchange (RMCE) in human cells and cancer-selective
expression systems, published in *Nucleic Acids Research*, *Scientific Reports*,
*Oncotarget* and *Gene Therapy*.

### Research context and significance

Current genome editors create a double-strand break and rely on cellular repair.
This works well for disruption and small edits, but has two structural
limitations:

1. **Inserting large sequences is inefficient.** Many disease-causing genes are
   too big to deliver by the routes that work best today.
2. **Breaks carry risk.** Indels, translocations, and activation of neighbouring
   genes are consequences of the break itself, not of imprecise targeting.

Tyrosine site-specific recombinases sidestep both. They bind a defined sequence
and catalyse integration directly — no break, and no dependence on the cell's
repair machinery. Because the human genome already contains tens of thousands of
sequences resembling the natural attB target, these enzymes can be directed to
sites the genome provides rather than sites we must first create.

The therapeutic consequence is that a **single construct can restore function
across a patient population carrying many different mutations in the same gene**,
rather than requiring a mutation-specific edit for each individual.

### Project directions

Your thesis will sit in one of the following, chosen with your interests in mind:

**1 · Recombinase engineering and directed evolution**
Generate and screen variants of HK022 and related integrases for higher activity
and tighter specificity at human genomic sites. Mutagenesis, selection systems,
and structure-guided design.

**2 · Therapeutic genome integration**
Develop and test integration strategies for hereditary disease or cancer —
including safe-harbour insertion, promoter-trap approaches, and replacement of
mutated sequence with functional donor DNA.

**3 · Cell-line engineering**
Build stable, engineered human cell lines and reporter systems: the models the
rest of the lab's work depends on, and a service we provide to collaborators.

**4 · Computational site discovery**
Mine genomes for new recombinase families and map their target sites; support
enzyme design with structural modelling.

### Techniques you will learn

Molecular cloning (Gibson, restriction, Golden Gate) · mammalian cell culture and
transfection · fluorescent reporter assays · flow cytometry / FACS · PCR, qPCR and
genotyping · NGS library preparation and analysis · protein expression and
purification · sequence analysis and structural modelling.
